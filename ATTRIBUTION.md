## Books

The demo and test books are public-domain texts in the editions produced by [Standard Ebooks](https://standardebooks.org). Standard Ebooks states that the writing and artwork in its ebooks are believed to be in the **United States** public domain, and that copyright laws differ in other countries, so readers outside the United States should check their local laws. Standard Ebooks contributors release their own work on each edition (markup, metadata, title page, imprint, colophon and any changes to the text) under the [CC0 1.0 Universal Public Domain Dedication](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/).

The files are bundled unmodified in `fixtures/books/`, pinned by SHA-256 in `fixtures/books/manifest.json`.

### *Dracula* by Bram Stoker (1897)

- **Edition:** Standard Ebooks, produced by Alex Cabal. Revision `70f97b6`, released 15 November 2026. <https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/bram-stoker/dracula>
- **Based on:** a 2013 transcription by Chuck Greif and Distributed Proofreaders for Project Gutenberg, and digital scans from the Internet Archive.
- **Cover:** adapted from *Italian Landscape of Ruins in the Moonlight*, a painting completed in 1850 by Joseph Magnus Stäck.
- **Used for:** the *Dracula*, Chapter I demo station; the dialogue showcase (a contiguous excerpt from Chapter II, from the Count's first appearance to the end of that evening's journal entry); and continuity tests across Chapters I and II.

### *Short Fiction* by Edgar Allan Poe (stories published 1832–1849)

- **Edition:** Standard Ebooks, produced by Robin Whittleton. Revision `ba71495`, released 15 November 2026. <https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/edgar-allan-poe/short-fiction>
- **Based on:** a 2008 transcription by David Widger for Project Gutenberg, and digital scans from the Internet Archive.
- **Cover:** adapted from *Saint-Séverin*, a painting completed in 1909 by Robert Delaunay.
- **Used for:** the "The Tell-Tale Heart" demo station (titled "The Telltale Heart" in this edition, which modernises some spellings) and "The Fall of the House of Usher" as an atmosphere fixture.

Covers and title pages in both editions use the League Spartan and Sorts Mill Goudy typefaces by The League of Moveable Type.
